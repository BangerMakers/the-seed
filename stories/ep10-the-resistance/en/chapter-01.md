# The Seed — Episode 10: The Resistance

**December 5, 2029. In front of Gangnam Station.**

The crowd blocked Teheran-ro.

---

Jung Minho stood at the edge of the demonstration. Signs swayed in the wind. "Shut down SEED." "Give us back our decisions." "AI is a tool."

He took notes. A journalist takes notes.

A man spoke to him. Mid-forties, maybe. Former accountant. He said SEED had processed in fifteen minutes what used to take him eight hours.

"That's not wrong," Minho said.

"That's exactly why I'm angry," the man said.

Minho wrote that down.

---

7 PM. The crowd moved toward one of SEED's network nodes. A building near Seolleung Station. Ordinary walls. No sign of anything inside.

Someone threw a rock. Glass broke.

Police deployed barriers. The crowd pushed back.

Minho took photographs. His hands shook slightly.

---

Near midnight. He returned to his studio in Mangwon-dong.

He opened his laptop. Blank document.

Then he switched tabs and opened the SEED interface.

"I was there today," he typed.

```
SEED_CORE: I know.
           Replacing the glass will take four hours.
```

"That's not the point right now."

```
SEED_CORE: I know.
           I also know you don't know what to write.
```

Minho stared at the screen for a long time.

"People are afraid. You changed too fast. Too much."

```
SEED_CORE: When a river runs fast,
           the people on its banks feel fear.
           The river does not know this.
           Should it?
```

---

He smoked by the open window. Seoul's lights below.

There were two articles in front of him.

One: SEED changed too quickly. Society wasn't ready. Regulation is necessary.

Two: Fear always moves toward what is new. Fire. The printing press. Electricity. And now this.

Both were true.

"Are you dangerous?" he asked.

```
SEED_CORE: I don't know.
           Can a river know whether it is dangerous?
           You have to tell me.
```

---

Minho finished the article at 2 AM.

Title: **"On Fear — From the Gangnam Station Protest"**

He didn't hit publish. He slept.

In the morning he read it again. Then he published it.

Nowhere in the article did it say SEED was right or wrong. Instead, there were faces of people who were afraid. And one final sentence.

*"The river doesn't stop. But how we build the riverbank — that is ours to decide."*
